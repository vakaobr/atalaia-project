Roadmap

Six phases, each closed by a real field test, its gate. The software has gone ahead of the hardware: phases 1 and 2 are done in software, the first board already plays Bomb on the bench and the gates wait for built boxes; phase 3 is nearly complete in software, and phase 4 has started.

  1. Done

    0 · Foundations

    Shared core, Lua sandbox, Skirmesh-RF codec, browser simulator.

    Gate: the simulator runs Domination and Bomb from Lua scripts. Met.

  2. Software done

    1 · Single box

    Field Box build, ESP32 firmware, on-device presets, and a build manual with a wiring diagram.

    Gate: one box runs a 2-hour club game with no restarts. The firmware and its screens are done and run in an emulator, and the build manual with its wiring diagrams is written. The first board (Heltec V4.3, 20 October 2026) boots, powers the radio, reads its buttons and plays Bomb (arming, defusing, the timer running out) on its own small screen; the gate stays open until a built box runs the 2 hours.

  3. Software done

    2 · Field network

    Skirmesh-RF over LoRa, field controller, API, GM console, and a mode pack, now written and tested in software: Hold-All Domination (score only while holding every box), Last Stand (a countdown to defeat while holding one of three), per-team arming codes on Bomb, and the Codes modifier (keypad codes that grant points, hints or unlocks, with the GM told who used them).

    Gate: 5 boxes on a CQB site and an outdoor field, zero lost captures. The encrypted radio protocol, the field controller with its API, the USB radio modem, the organisers' console with a scoreboard, joining a game with an NFC card or a QR code, and the boxes' firmware talking to the controller are built and tested in the simulator, the emulator and on a computer, whole games included. The gate needs the radio boards.

  4. In progress

    3 · Android and players

    Android props, the I/O pod, the app for players, marshals and the GM, and the modes that need players' phones and tags.

    Done in software (plan phases A to F): Kill Confirmed, Respawn and Lives and Player Check-in, which read players' signed NFC tags, and the Medic Kit (a hit player scans their tag and holds a button to be healed, with a limited number of heals); mode screens described in YAML, drawn alike by the Field Box and the app; chrono check-in (energy, limits per class, spot checks, readings signed by the marshal). The app runs the same game core as the boxes, in Portuguese and English: an Android phone acts as a box (any mode, with the mode's own screens, big team buttons, a keypad and NFC tags) and joins a game by QR code, NFC card or text; players follow the game; marshals have the chrono, the replica registry and players' tags; the GM opens the console. Players check in at the field by scanning a printed poster (no account, no internet), say which replicas they will use and see whether they have passed the chrono; marshals work from that "replicas to chrono" list, and their signed readings mark each replica passed or refused. Players can share their position during a game, and the controller decides who sees it. An Android prop takes part in the controller's game over the field's Wi-Fi, or over its radio through an I/O pod, whose firmware lends the phone a Heltec board's team buttons, keypad, lights, siren, NFC reader, enclosure switch and LoRa radio over USB or Bluetooth. A field or club that runs its own controller can show its logo, wallpaper and colour in the app, on the scoreboard and on the poster, and give its teams names and crests.

    Still to come. In software: the phone as the field controller (its MQTT broker and position filter are built, the controller itself is not) and the iOS version (not built: it needs a Mac); the Money Factory mode is deferred. Plan phase G, the field test with real phones and pods, waits for the hardware: so far the app has run only on Android emulators, and the pod firmware only as its own code on a computer.

    Gate: one game mixes ESP32 and Android props without issues.

  5. In progress

    4 · Site server and advanced play

    Site server (accounts, game history, the chrono registry, scheduling games, registration and publishing), custom scenarios, advanced modes (master and satellite zones, VIP escort, hacking terminals and more) and ATAK. The research, the architecture and the implementation plan are written, and the plan is being built in small steps.

    Done in software and tested on a computer, not yet on real boxes or a real server. Modes: Master and Satellites (satellites held with the master raise its points; the master changing hands frees them), the Hacking Terminal (a puzzle on the keypad that reveals a code or captures the terminal), VIP / Payload (a team escorts its VIP or a phone with GPS to an extraction zone; checkpoint boxes score while held; positions stay on the field controller and the phone), the runtime of custom scenarios (a scenario runs on the field controller and tells the boxes of the built-in objectives package what to be: a point to capture, a code, tags or cards to scan, a bomb site; they report, the scenario scores), the scenario compiler (a YAML scenario of seven objective types, with dependencies, reveals and time windows, checked with every problem located and explained, and compiled into such a scenario from the command line), scenarios in the GM console and the app (the console's scenario editor lists every problem as the organiser types and installs the scenario; the boxes get the scenario's names when they join; players see in the app the objectives meant for them, with their names and how each stands, a hidden one only once it is revealed), the rules of Target Windows (a hit while the target shows a team's colour scores for that team; the target addon is not built yet), geo utilities in the core (distances and zones for every script, identical on every platform), and the ATAK feed (the boxes and zones on ATAK maps over the field Wi-Fi, in their owners' colours, placed by giving two corners of the field image their map position; player positions never go to everyone, only to staff devices when the field turns it on; not yet tried with a real ATAK phone). Site server: its skeleton (database, migrations, health check, in its own container), accounts (passwords, two-factor codes required for organisers and marshals, sessions and roles per field) and personal data rights (access, export, correction, erasure, consent records, a daily retention purge), and the controller sync (field controllers push their games, logs and scores to the site server, offline first and safe to replay; not yet over a real 4G link), and the chrono registry (signed readings verified and kept as signed, public keys only, sharing between fields with consent; not yet with a real chronograph), and game scheduling with a public page per field and open iCalendar and JSON feeds of its games (no personal data on them), and registration for those games with pre-declared replicas and a check-in ticket: the field controller pulls the list and reports who came; registering from the app, and importing a registration list (CSV or JSON) exported from another platform; and club profiles (name, colour, logo and crest, members, a public page off by default with a report button and moderation) with the field's branding and saved teams synced to its controllers, so a club's crest reaches every field that saved its team; and device health (each box's battery, link and firmware from what the controllers send), a firmware catalogue that only lists releases signed with a trusted key (nothing is installed: over-the-air updates are not built yet), and the remote GM: an organiser with the GM role, logged in with a two-factor code, sees the live game and starts, pauses, resumes or ends it, or sends a message or a mode command, over a connection the field controller opens itself, only when the field turns it on, with every command logged on both sides; and game history (each game's score timeline and event log; players and spectators see a live game a minute behind, without secrets or player ids), a public scoreboard per field (off by default) and the field's scenario library with its game presets, checked as the field controller checks them and installed by every controller of the field, a standby included.

    Gate: a weekend milsim runs on Atalaia end to end.

  6. Designed

    5 · Community

    Visual mode editor, mode registry, carrier PCB (a ready-made circuit board that makes builds easier), translations, build guides for other node types. The research, the architecture and the implementation plan are written; there is no code yet.

    Ongoing after the first release.
